I study how public institutions work in practice.

And how their spaces, rules, technologies, people, and processes shape policy implementation.

I am Igor Caires Machado, a researcher working at the intersection of judicial governance, public policy, and sociomateriality. My trajectory began with judicial architecture and now extends to regulation, judicial education, and the concrete conditions through which institutional decisions become action.

TRAJECTORY

From the architecture of institutions to the study of how they act.

My interest in judicial buildings led to a broader question: how do spaces, rules, technologies, and people shape the functioning of public organizations? This question connects my research on judicial architecture, implementation, governance, and judicial education.

BACKGROUND

Institutional spaces

Architecture and experience with planning, projects, and infrastructure provided the first lens: observing how material design shapes uses, flows, and capacities.

PHD RESEARCH · 2024

From form to practice

With ARQJUD, the judicial building becomes a research problem: built environment, workplace experience, recognition, access, and functionality.

POSTDOCTORAL RESEARCH · ENAP

Organizations and implementation

The question expands from the isolated building to arrangements of people, rules, technologies, and infrastructure that participate in implementation.

CURRENT AGENDA

Governance, regulation, and learning

Regulation, intermediaries, institutional capacities, coordination, and learning become part of an integrated agenda on how public institutions act and learn.
