Core question

What changes when the analysis of regulation includes the intermediaries that translate, certify, monitor, and verify rules?

Three stages, three units of analysis

The design separates documentary screening, candidate adjudication, and normalized RIT relationships. This reduces ambiguity and ontological mixing.

01

Screening

Structured identification of candidate occurrences and evidence in regulatory acts and documents.

02

Relational adjudication

Determining when a regulator–intermediary–target relationship is empirically supported.

03

Institutional coding

Normalizing actors, roles, and mechanisms to enable comparison across episodes and cases.

From proposal to comparable data

The project moved from a broad conceptual map to a reproducible empirical architecture with explicit control over the unit of analysis.

  1. R1

    Initial mapping

    Selection and reading of acts, case identification, and first intermediation episodes.

  2. R2

    Relational normalization

    One row per relationship, actor lookup tables, and separation of documentary evidence, adjudication, and coding.

  3. NOW

    Harmonization and comparison

    Consolidating episodes and preparing them for comparison with collaborators’ datasets and coding.
